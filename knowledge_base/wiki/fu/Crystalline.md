# [FU] Crystalline

**来源**: Frackin' Universe Wiki | **链接**: https://frackinuniverse.miraheze.org/wiki/Crystalline

---

**Crystalline** worlds are made almost entirely out of crystal. The crystal ground tiles here will yield precious silicon when tossed into the extractor. Lunari Crystal trees also dot the landscape and can be easily identified by the semi-transparent "bubbles" on the branches. Chopping these down will drop Lunari Shards which can then be combined into the full Lunari Crystals to be used in crafting gear.

> Appearance : Colors can vary pretty wildly on crystalline moons, but unless you're in a sub-biome, everything you see will be made of crystals. Even the trees and "plant" life are made of crystals. You'll also encounter ice pretty commonly as well as plenty of Organic Soup that will restore your hunger.

> Dangers : While not recommended when just starting off, Crystalline worlds tend to be relatively safe. You will occasionally have to deal with ice, crystal and electric storms, but otherwise the monsters are the biggest threat. Crystalline worlds tend to fall around an "average" threat level, making them fairly accessible without endgame gear.

> Navigation Console :
> * Sure, they are really cold. Sure, they aren't comfortable camping. But they are a rich source of silicon, crystals and that sort of thing. Watch out for the crystal rain, though. Ow.

> Notable Features : The occasional underground deposit of Cinnabar, and Prism Shards makes this more profitable. They are also one of only two biomes where Fenerox can naturally be found. 
> Despite the Navigation Console telling you that these planets are cold, plus the prevalence of ice, no cold protection is required (this is a holdover from older days. See below.)

> Trivia :
> Once upon a time, Crystalline were moons (much like the Rocky or Shadow Moons) and had the same rules (Airlessness, reduced gravity, ghost, meteorites, and all) before being graduated to full on planets. In fact, internally they are still referred to as 'Crystalmoons'.
